Guide wrapping is one of those things that looks simple until you try it. Getting the tension consistent, the thread colour right and the epoxy coat level takes practice — but understanding the basics helps you make better choices when speccing a build.

## Thread weight and colour

Most rod wraps use size A or size D nylon thread. Size A is finer and works well for lightweight builds or decorative patterns; size D is more robust and better suited to heavy-action rods.

Colour-wise, you're not limited — we stock a wide range of solids and can combine colours for contrast wraps or chevron patterns. Just bear in mind that lighter colours can become slightly translucent once epoxy is applied.

## Guide frame material

The three most common options:

| Material | Weight | Durability | Best for |
|----------|--------|------------|----------|
| Stainless steel | Heavier | Very good | Budget builds, coarse/carp |
| Titanium | Lightest | Excellent | Lightweight, performance builds |
| SIC insert | Standard | Excellent | All-round, spinning, lure |

For most builds we default to SIC-insert guides in titanium frames — they're light, durable and handle braided line without grooving.

## Spacing and number of guides

This is where blank knowledge matters. Too few guides and the blank doesn't load properly; too many adds unnecessary weight. We position guides by feel as well as calculation — watching how the blank bends under load and adjusting accordingly.

## The epoxy coat

A two-part epoxy is applied over the thread wraps and rotated on a drying motor until cured. The result is a hard, glossy finish that protects the thread from abrasion and moisture.

One coat is standard. Two coats gives a slightly thicker, more domed finish that some builders prefer aesthetically.
